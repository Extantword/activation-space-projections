SUPERPOSED HARMONIC MODES (MULTI-MODAL WAVE FIELD)
===================================================

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
A dataset of 64x64 grayscale images of complex wave fields formed by
adding 2-5 angular-radial harmonic modes together.  Each mode is
    A * cos(n * theta + phi) * sin(freq * r * pi)
and the sum of several such modes with different angular orders and
radial frequencies creates richly textured, often asymmetric wave
patterns that combine elements of all the individual harmonics.


2. GENERATION ALGORITHM
-----------------------
For each image:

  1. Pre-compute polar coordinates from the image centre:
         r, theta

  2. Choose  num_modes  in  {2, 3, 4, 5}.

  3. For each mode, randomise:
         n_mode  in  {0, ..., 8}       (angular order)
         freq    in  [0.15, 0.8]       (radial frequency)
         phase   in  [0, 2*pi)
         amp     in  [0.3, 1.0]

  4. Sum all modes:
         pattern = sum_k  amp_k * cos(n_k * theta + phase_k)
                                * sin(freq_k * r * pi)

  5. Apply circular envelope:
         pattern *= clamp(1 - r / max_r, 0, 1)

  6. Normalise to [0, 255].


3. MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND
---------------------------
Symmetry:  varies — depends on the GCD of the angular mode numbers.

If the angular orders are {n_1, n_2, ..., n_K}, the symmetry of the
sum is  C_g  where  g = GCD(n_1, ..., n_K).  Special cases:
  - All modes have n = 0: SO(2) (radially symmetric)
  - GCD = 1: C_1 (no rotational symmetry, but still structured)
  - A single dominant mode: approximate C_n for that mode

The sum is an element of the function space spanned by the
"cylindrical harmonics" — the eigenfunctions of the Laplacian in
polar coordinates.  This is the standard Fourier decomposition used
in solving the wave equation on a disk.

Connection to physics:  The individual modes are vibrational modes
of a circular drum (Experiment 16).  Their superposition represents
a general vibration state — the instantaneous displacement of a drum
struck at an arbitrary point.

The superposition principle means the resulting field can exhibit:
  - Constructive interference (bright spots where modes align)
  - Destructive interference (dark regions where modes cancel)
  - Complex nodal patterns (where the sum crosses zero)

Parameter space:  (num_modes, {n_k, freq_k, phase_k, amp_k}) —
variable-dimensional: 4 * num_modes + 1 continuous parameters.
