BILATERAL BUTTERFLY
===================

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
A dataset of 64x64 grayscale images of butterfly-like shapes.  Each image
consists of several cubic Bezier curves drawn on the right half of the canvas
and then mirrored pixel-for-pixel across the vertical centre axis.  The result
is exact bilateral (left-right) symmetry, evoking the wing patterns of
butterflies or Rorschach inkblots.


2. GENERATION ALGORITHM
-----------------------
For each image:

  1. Let  cx = size // 2  be the vertical axis of symmetry.

  2. Choose a random number of curves (between 2 and 5).

  3. For each curve, define four control points for a cubic Bezier:

       - Endpoints p0 and p3 are placed ON the axis:
             p0 = (cx, y0),    y0  uniform in [5, size-5]
             p3 = (cx, y3),    y3  uniform in [5, size-5]

       - Control points p1 and p2 are placed on the RIGHT half:
             p1 = (x1, y1),   x1 in [cx, size-1],  y1 in [0, size-1]
             p2 = (x2, y2),   x2 in [cx, size-1],  y2 in [0, size-1]

  4. Evaluate the cubic Bezier at 51 uniformly spaced parameter values
     t in [0, 1] to produce the right-side polyline:

         B(t) = (1-t)^3 * p0  +  3(1-t)^2 t * p1
                + 3(1-t) t^2 * p2  +  t^3 * p3

  5. Create the mirrored (left-side) polyline by reflecting every point
     across the axis:
         (x, y)  ->  (2*cx - x, y)

  6. Draw both polylines (white on black).


3. MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND
---------------------------
Symmetry group:  Z2  (cyclic group of order 2, generated by a single
                       reflection)

The sole non-identity element is the reflection

    sigma : (x, y)  ->  (2*cx - x, y)

which satisfies  sigma^2 = id.  The group is isomorphic to Z/2Z.

Cubic Bezier curves are polynomial parametric curves of degree 3.  The
general form is:
    B(t) = sum_{i=0}^{3}  C(3,i) (1-t)^{3-i} t^i  P_i ,    t in [0,1]

Because the endpoints lie on the mirror axis and the control points are
reflected, the pair of curves (right + left) forms a single piecewise-smooth
closed contour that is invariant under sigma.

The space of images in this dataset is parameterised by:
  - 2 endpoint y-coordinates  (y0, y3)
  - 4 control-point coordinates  (x1, y1, x2, y2)
per curve, times 2-5 curves per image.  This gives a variable-dimensional
parameter space (12 to 30 real numbers), all lying within the manifold of
bilaterally symmetric images.

Bilateral symmetry is the most common symmetry in biology (Bilateria).  It
is characterised by a single mirror plane, and is the simplest non-trivial
spatial symmetry possible in 2D.
