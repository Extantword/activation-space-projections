DOUBLE-SLIT INTERFERENCE (COHERENT TWO-SOURCE FRINGES)
======================================================

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
A dataset of 64x64 grayscale images showing the interference pattern
from two coherent point sources (the "double slit" or Young's
experiment).  The intensity at each pixel is the squared sum of two
circular waves, producing smooth hyperbolic fringe lines with
bilateral symmetry about both the axis connecting the sources and the
perpendicular bisector.


2. GENERATION ALGORITHM
-----------------------
For each image:

  1. Place two point sources symmetrically on the vertical axis:
         S1 = (cx, cy - d/2)
         S2 = (cx, cy + d/2)
     where  d  is the slit separation.

  2. Randomise:
         d  (slit separation)  in  [0.05*size, 0.3*size]
         k  (wavenumber)       in  [0.5, 2.0]

  3. For every pixel (x, y), compute:
         r1 = distance to S1
         r2 = distance to S2
         I(x,y) = 2 + 2 * cos(k * (r1 - r2))

     This is  |e^{ikr1} + e^{ikr2}|^2  for unit-amplitude waves.

  4. Normalise to [0, 255].


3. MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND
---------------------------
Symmetry:  D2  (two perpendicular mirror axes)

The path-length difference  delta = r1 - r2  determines the fringe
pattern.  Constructive interference (bright) occurs when:
    delta = m * lambda     (m integer,  lambda = 2*pi/k)

Destructive interference (dark) when:
    delta = (m + 1/2) * lambda

The locus of constant  delta  is a hyperbola with foci at S1 and S2.
The fringe pattern is therefore a family of confocal hyperbolas.

Far from the sources (Fraunhofer regime), the fringes become equally-
spaced parallel lines with spacing:
    fringe_spacing = lambda * L / d

where L is the distance to the observation plane and d is the slit
separation.  Our computation uses the exact near-field (Fresnel)
formula, which shows curved fringes near the sources.

This is the setup of Young's double-slit experiment (1801), which
first demonstrated the wave nature of light.

Parameter space:  (d, k) — a 2-D continuous space.  The product k*d
determines the fringe density; the ratio d/size controls the
curvature of the hyperbolic fringes.
