SYMMETRIC CONTOUR MAPS
======================

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
A dataset of 64x64 grayscale images of topographic-style contour lines.
Gaussian blobs are placed with D_n symmetry, their sum forms a smooth
2-D field, and iso-contour lines at evenly-spaced thresholds are
highlighted, producing concentric closed curves that inherit the D_n
symmetry of the underlying field.


2. GENERATION ALGORITHM
-----------------------
For each image:

  1. Choose  fold = n  from  {3, 4, 5, 6}.
  2. Place  num_base  (2-5) Gaussian blobs in the fundamental wedge,
     then replicate by D_n (n rotations x 2 mirrors).
  3. Sum all Gaussians into a smooth field and normalise to [0, 1].
  4. Choose  num_levels  (5-10) contour thresholds.
  5. For each threshold, highlight pixels where the field value is
     near the threshold using a narrow Gaussian kernel:
         contour(x,y) = exp( -((field(x,y) - level) / 0.02)^2 )
  6. Sum all contour contributions, normalise to [0, 255].


3. MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND
---------------------------
Symmetry group:  D_n  (dihedral, n rotations + n reflections)

The contour lines of a function with D_n symmetry are themselves
D_n-symmetric curves.  At each threshold, the iso-line is either a
single closed curve or a union of closed curves.  As the threshold
increases toward the maximum, the curves shrink toward the local
maxima of the field.

Topological transitions (contour merging/splitting) occur at saddle
points of the field, corresponding to the Morse theory of the
Gaussian-mixture function.

The rendering technique (narrow Gaussian at each level) is equivalent
to computing  delta(field - level)  convolved with a smoothing kernel,
which approximates the mathematical definition of a contour as a
level set.

Parameter space:  (n, blob positions and sizes) — similar to
Experiment 24 but with contour-line rendering instead of raw field
display.
