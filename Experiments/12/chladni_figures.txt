CHLADNI FIGURES
===============

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
A dataset of 64x64 grayscale images showing the nodal-line patterns of
a vibrating square plate.  These are the patterns Ernst Chladni observed
in 1787 by bowing a sand-covered metal plate: sand collects along the
nodal lines (zero-displacement curves) of the vibration modes.


2. GENERATION ALGORITHM
-----------------------
For each image:

  1. Normalise pixel coordinates to [0, 1]:
         x_norm = x / size,   y_norm = y / size

  2. Choose two integer mode numbers:
         m  in  {1, 2, ..., 8}
         n  in  {1, 2, ..., 8}

  3. Choose the superposition sign:  s  in  {+1, -1}.

  4. Compute the plate function at every pixel:

         f(x,y) = cos(m*pi*x) * cos(n*pi*y)
                  + s * cos(n*pi*x) * cos(m*pi*y)

  5. Highlight the nodal lines (where f is near zero) using a Gaussian
     kernel:

         pixel_value = 255 * exp( -(f / w)^2 )

     with width parameter  w = 0.15.

     Pixels near f = 0 are bright; pixels far from zero are dark.


3. MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND
---------------------------
The vibration of a thin elastic plate is governed by the biharmonic
equation:
    D * nabla^4 w  +  rho * h * (d^2 w/dt^2) = 0

For a square plate with free edges, the normal modes are well
approximated by products of cosines:

    w_mn(x,y,t) = [ cos(m*pi*x/L) * cos(n*pi*y/L)
                    +/- cos(n*pi*x/L) * cos(m*pi*y/L) ] * cos(omega_mn * t)

The nodal lines are the curves where  w_mn = 0.  Their geometry depends
on the mode numbers (m, n) and the sign:

  - With "+":  the pattern has the full symmetry of the square, D4
    (4 rotations + 4 mirrors).
  - With "-":  the diagonal mirror symmetries are broken, leaving D2
    (2 rotations + 2 mirrors), but new nodal lines along the diagonals
    appear.
  - When m = n:
    - With "+":  f = 2 * cos(m*pi*x) * cos(m*pi*y), giving a grid
      of squares (D4 symmetry).
    - With "-":  f = 0 everywhere (trivial mode).

The number of nodal lines roughly increases with m + n.  The pattern
complexity grows quadratically with the mode numbers.

Chladni figures were historically important as the first systematic
study of 2-D wave phenomena and led to the development of plate
vibration theory by Sophie Germain (1816).

Parameter space:  (m, n, sign) — a finite discrete space of
8 * 8 * 2 = 128 possible modes (with some symmetry-related
degeneracies, since swapping m and n with sign=+1 gives the same
pattern).
