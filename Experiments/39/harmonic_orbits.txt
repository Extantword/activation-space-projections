HARMONIC ORBITS (MULTI-FREQUENCY CIRCULAR MOTION)
==================================================

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
A dataset of 64x64 grayscale images of curves traced by the sum of
3-5 circular motions at different integer frequencies:
    z(t) = sum_k  A_k * exp(i * f_k * t)
This generalises the 2-frequency spirograph (Experiments 6, 14, 15)
to higher-order harmonic combinations, producing intricate, often
self-intersecting closed curves.


2. GENERATION ALGORITHM
-----------------------
For each image:

  1. Choose  K  harmonics  in  {3, 4, 5}.
  2. Sample K distinct integer frequencies from {1, ..., 14}.
  3. For each harmonic, choose:
       amplitude  in  [0.3, 1.0]
       phase      in  [0, 2*pi)
  4. Evaluate at 2001 points:
       x(t) = sum_k  A_k * cos(f_k * t + phi_k)
       y(t) = sum_k  A_k * sin(f_k * t + phi_k)
  5. Scale to fit and draw as a polyline.


3. MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND
---------------------------
Symmetry:  C_g  where  g = GCD(f_1, f_2, ..., f_K)

A sum of circular motions is a finite trigonometric polynomial in
the complex plane.  The resulting curve is always closed (periodic
with period 2*pi / GCD).

When all frequencies are coprime (GCD = 1), the only symmetry is
the identity — the curve has C_1 (no non-trivial rotational symmetry).
When g = GCD > 1, the curve has C_g symmetry because replacing t by
t + 2*pi/g maps each term exp(i*f_k*t) to exp(i*f_k*t) * exp(2*pi*i*f_k/g),
and since g divides every f_k, each phase factor is 1.

The number of self-intersections can be very large.  For K harmonics
with frequencies f_1 < ... < f_K, an upper bound on the number of
double points is proportional to (sum of frequencies)^2.

Connection to Fourier series:  Any closed planar curve can be
decomposed as a sum of circular motions (its Fourier series).  Our
images use a finite truncation with randomly chosen harmonics,
sampling the space of all closed curves.

The spirograph (K=2) traces an epitrochoid or hypotrochoid; K >= 3
harmonics produce qualitatively richer curves with more crossing
patterns and symmetry-breaking possibilities.

Parameter space:  (K, {f_k, A_k, phi_k}) — variable-dimensional:
3*K + 1 parameters (3 per harmonic plus K itself).
