STAR POLYGONS
=============

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
A dataset of 64x64 grayscale images of star polygons — figures formed by
connecting every k-th vertex of a regular n-gon.  Each image may contain
one to three nested star polygons at different scales and with different
step sizes, producing complex rotationally-symmetric figures.


2. GENERATION ALGORITHM
-----------------------
For each image:

  1. Place the centre at (cx, cy) = (size/2, size/2).

  2. Randomise the structure parameters:
       - n_vertices     in  {5, 6, 7, ..., 15}
       - num_stars       in  {1, 2, 3}
       - base_rotation   in  [0, 2*pi)

  3. For each star layer  s = 0, 1, ..., num_stars-1:

       a. Compute the circumradius:
              r_s = (size/2 - 2) * (1 - s * 0.25)
          If r_s < 5 pixels, skip.

       b. Choose the step size:
              k  in  [2,  max(2, n_vertices//2 - 1)]

       c. Apply a small rotation offset per layer:
              rotation_s = base_rotation + s * random(0, pi/n_vertices)

       d. Compute the vertices of the regular n-gon:
              V_j = ( cx + r_s * cos(rotation_s + 2*pi*j/n) ,
                      cy + r_s * sin(rotation_s + 2*pi*j/n) )
          for j = 0, 1, ..., n-1.

       e. For each vertex j, draw a line from V_j to V_{(j+k) mod n}.


3. MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND
---------------------------
Symmetry group:  C_n  or  D_n  (depending on parameters)

A single star polygon {n/k} is the figure obtained by connecting every
k-th vertex of a regular n-gon.  It has the full dihedral symmetry D_n
(n rotations + n reflections).

The Schlafli symbol {n/k} denotes a star polygon with:
  - n vertices equally spaced on a circle
  - Each vertex connected to the vertex k positions ahead

For {n/k} to form a single connected star (rather than a compound of
several polygons), gcd(n, k) must equal 1.  When gcd(n, k) = d > 1,
the figure decomposes into d separate {n/d, k/d} polygons rotated
relative to each other.

Examples:
  - {5/2}:  the classic pentagram (five-pointed star)
  - {6/2}:  hexagram = two overlapping triangles (Star of David)
  - {7/2}:  heptagram
  - {7/3}:  a more "pointy" heptagram
  - {8/3}:  octagram

The interior angle at each point of a {n/k} star polygon is:
    alpha = 180 * (n - 2k) / n   degrees

The total turning number (winding number around the centre) is k.

When multiple star layers are nested with slight rotation offsets, the
individual D_n symmetries of each layer are generally broken, leaving
only the common C_n rotational symmetry.  If all layers share the same
rotation, the full D_n is preserved.

Star polygons appear throughout history in art, architecture, and
religious symbolism (Islamic geometric art, the Star of David, the
Seal of Solomon, Kepler's stellated polyhedra).  They are also related
to modular arithmetic:  the sequence of vertices visited by stepping
by k positions is the coset  {0, k, 2k, ...}  modulo n.

Parameter space:  Each image is determined by (n_vertices, num_stars,
base_rotation, k per layer, rotation offset per layer).  The discrete
parameter n_vertices partitions the dataset into 11 symmetry classes
(n = 5 through 15).  Within each class, the choice of k and the nesting
create further visual diversity.
