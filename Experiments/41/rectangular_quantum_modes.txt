RECTANGULAR QUANTUM MODES (PARTICLE-IN-A-BOX)
==============================================

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
A dataset of 64x64 grayscale images showing the probability density
of a quantum particle confined to a 2D square well.  Each image
displays  |psi_{m,n}(x,y)|^2 = sin^2(m*pi*x) * sin^2(n*pi*y),
producing a smooth, grid-like intensity pattern where bright regions
indicate high probability and dark nodal lines indicate zero
probability.


2. GENERATION ALGORITHM
-----------------------
For each image:

  1. Normalise pixel coordinates to [0, 1]:
         x_norm = x / size,   y_norm = y / size

  2. Choose mode numbers:  m, n  in  {1, ..., 8}.

  3. Compute the probability density at every pixel:
         |psi|^2 = sin^2(m * pi * x_norm) * sin^2(n * pi * y_norm)

  4. Scale to [0, 255]  (the max of sin^2 * sin^2 is always 1).


3. MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND
---------------------------
Symmetry:  D2 when m != n;  D4 when m = n.

The stationary states of a particle in a 2D infinite square well
are separable products of 1D solutions:

    psi_{m,n}(x,y) = (2/L) * sin(m*pi*x/L) * sin(n*pi*y/L)

with energies  E_{m,n} = (pi^2 * hbar^2 / (2 * mass * L^2)) * (m^2 + n^2).

The nodal lines (where psi = 0) form a rectangular grid:
  - Vertical lines at  x = k/m  for k = 0, 1, ..., m
  - Horizontal lines at  y = k/n  for k = 0, 1, ..., n

The number of nodal cells (bright rectangles) is  m * n.

Degeneracy:  states (m, n) and (n, m) have the same energy when m != n
but different nodal patterns, rotated by 90 degrees.  This is the
simplest example of quantum degeneracy.

Parameter space:  (m, n) — a 2D discrete lattice with 64 states.
The images form a visually smooth 2-parameter family.
