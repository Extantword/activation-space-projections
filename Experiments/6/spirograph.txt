SPIROGRAPH (HYPOTROCHOID)
=========================

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
A dataset of 64x64 grayscale images of spirograph patterns — curves
generated by a point attached to a small circle rolling inside a larger
fixed circle.  These are mathematically known as hypotrochoids.  The
resulting patterns exhibit intricate rotational symmetry whose order
depends on the ratio of the two circle radii.


2. GENERATION ALGORITHM
-----------------------
For each image:

  1. Randomise the three curve parameters:
       - R  (fixed circle radius)  in  [15, 28]
       - r  (rolling circle radius) in  [3, 0.8*R]
       - d  (pen distance from rolling centre) in  [0.3*r, 1.2*r]

  2. Trace the curve over 20 full revolutions of the rolling circle,
     sampling 2000 points.  At parameter t:

       x(t) = (R - r) * cos(t)  +  d * cos( (R-r)*t / r )
       y(t) = (R - r) * sin(t)  -  d * sin( (R-r)*t / r )

  3. Compute the bounding box of all sampled points and rescale to fit
     within the image (with a 3-pixel margin on each side), centred.

  4. Draw the resulting polyline (white on black).


3. MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND
---------------------------
A hypotrochoid is the trace of a point at distance d from the centre of
a circle of radius r rolling without slipping inside a fixed circle of
radius R.  The parametric equations are:

    x(t) = (R - r) cos(t) + d cos((R - r)t / r)
    y(t) = (R - r) sin(t) - d sin((R - r)t / r)

Special cases:
  - d = r :  hypocycloid (the point is on the rim of the rolling circle)
  - R = 2r:  the curve degenerates to an ellipse (or a line if d = r)
  - R/r integer n:  the curve is an n-cusped hypocycloid (when d = r)

Rotational symmetry:  If R/r = p/q (in lowest terms), the curve closes
after q full turns of the rolling circle and has p-fold rotational
symmetry (C_p).  When R/r is irrational, the curve never closes and
densely fills an annular region.

Since R and r are chosen as continuous random values, R/r is almost
surely irrational.  However, over 20 revolutions the curve comes close
to closing, and the visual appearance is of approximate C_n symmetry
where n = round(R/r).

The Spirograph toy (invented 1965) uses gears with integer tooth counts,
guaranteeing rational R/r.  Our continuous version produces a richer
variety of near-symmetric patterns.

Fourier interpretation:  The hypotrochoid can be written as:

    z(t) = (R - r) * exp(it)  +  d * exp(-i(R-r)t/r)

in the complex plane.  This is a sum of two circular motions (epicycles)
with frequencies +1 and -(R-r)/r.  The interference of these two
frequencies is what creates the lobed, rotationally-symmetric pattern.

The maximum radial extent is  |R - r| + d  and the minimum is
| |R - r| - d |.  When d > |R-r|, the curve has inner loops.

Parameter space:  Each image is determined by (R, r, d) — a 3-dimensional
continuous space.  The scaling step makes the dataset invariant to global
size changes, so effectively only the ratios R:r:d matter (a 2D manifold).
