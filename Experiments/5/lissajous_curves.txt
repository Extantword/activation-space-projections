LISSAJOUS CURVES
================

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
A dataset of 64x64 grayscale images of Lissajous curves — closed
parametric curves defined by two sinusoidal oscillations in perpendicular
directions with integer frequency ratios.  Different frequency pairs
produce curves with distinct symmetry properties ranging from simple
ellipses to complex multi-lobed figures.


2. GENERATION ALGORITHM
-----------------------
For each image:

  1. Choose a frequency pair (a, b) uniformly at random from:
       (1,2), (1,3), (2,3), (3,4), (3,5),
       (4,5), (1,4), (2,5), (3,7), (5,6)

  2. Choose a phase offset:
       delta  in  [0, pi]

  3. Set amplitude = size/2 - 3 and centre = (size/2, size/2).

  4. Evaluate the parametric equations at 501 uniformly spaced values
     of  t  in  [0, 2*pi]:

       x(t) = cx + amplitude * sin(a*t + delta)
       y(t) = cy + amplitude * sin(b*t)

  5. Draw the resulting polyline (white on black).


3. MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND
---------------------------
Lissajous curves (also called Bowditch curves) are the family of curves:

    x = A * sin(a*t + delta)
    y = B * sin(b*t)

When a/b is rational (p/q in lowest terms), the curve closes after a
period of  T = 2*pi * lcm(1/a, 1/b).

Symmetry properties depend on the ratio a:b and the phase delta:

  - When delta = 0  and  a, b are both odd:
        The curve has two perpendicular mirror axes (D2 symmetry).

  - When delta = pi/2  and  a:b = 1:2:
        The curve traces a parabola-like figure with one mirror axis.

  - When delta = pi/(2a):
        The curve often has maximal symmetry for its frequency ratio.

  - General delta:
        Typically only the rotational symmetry R(pi) (180-degree rotation)
        survives when a and b are both odd, or no symmetry at all.

Number of lobes:  A Lissajous curve with frequencies (a, b) can have up
to  (2a-1)(2b-1)  intersection points and generally exhibits  a  lobes
in the x-direction and  b  lobes in the y-direction.

Special cases among the chosen pairs:
  - (1, 2):  Figure-eight or parabolic shape (2 lobes)
  - (1, 3):  Three-lobed curve
  - (2, 3):  The classic "pretzel" curve (Lissajous knot projection)
  - (3, 4):  Complex 12-crossing pattern

The phase parameter delta continuously deforms the curve, sweeping through
different symmetry classes.  At delta = 0, the curve degenerates to a line
segment; at delta = pi/2, it often achieves maximal area.

In physics, Lissajous curves describe the superposition of two
perpendicular simple harmonic motions and appear naturally on
oscilloscopes.  They are also related to the normal modes of a
rectangular vibrating membrane.

Parameter space:  Each image is parameterised by (a, b, delta) — a
2-dimensional continuous manifold (for each discrete frequency pair).
The 10 frequency pairs create 10 topologically distinct curve families
within the dataset.
