SNOWFLAKE DENDRITES
===================

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
A dataset of 64x64 grayscale images resembling snowflake crystals.  Each
image contains a single fractal-like branching tree that is replicated six
times at 60-degree intervals around the centre, producing six-fold (C6)
rotational symmetry — the hallmark of real ice crystals.


2. GENERATION ALGORITHM
-----------------------
For each image:

  1. Place the centre at (cx, cy) = (size/2, size/2).

  2. Randomise the global branch parameters:
       - trunk_length  in  [0.3*size, 0.45*size]
       - max_depth     in  {2, 3}

  3. Fix a random seed (branch_seed) so that the pseudo-random number
     generator produces the same sequence for every arm.

  4. For each of 6 arms  (k = 0, 1, ..., 5):

       a. Set the arm direction:  angle_k = k * pi/3

       b. Reset  random.seed(branch_seed)  so the recursion is identical.

       c. Call the recursive branching function:

          _draw_branch(draw, cx, cy, angle, length, depth, max_depth):
            - If depth > max_depth or length < 2: return.
            - Compute the endpoint:
                  ex = cx + length * cos(angle)
                  ey = cy + length * sin(angle)
            - Draw a line from (cx,cy) to (ex,ey).
            - Pick random sub-branch parameters:
                  branch_angle  in  [pi/6, pi/3]
                  shrink        in  [0.45, 0.65]
                  branch_pos    in  [0.4, 0.8]
            - Compute the mid-branch point:
                  bx = cx + length * branch_pos * cos(angle)
                  by = cy + length * branch_pos * sin(angle)
            - Recurse twice from (bx, by) at angles  angle +/- branch_angle
              with length * shrink.
            - Pick tip parameters:
                  tip_angle   in  [pi/8, pi/4]
                  tip_shrink  in  [0.3, 0.5]
            - Recurse twice from (ex, ey) at angles  angle +/- tip_angle
              with length * tip_shrink.

  5. Restore true randomness (random.seed()) before the next image.


3. MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND
---------------------------
Symmetry group:  C6  (cyclic group of order 6)

C6 is generated by a single rotation of 60 degrees:
    C6 = { R(0), R(pi/3), R(2pi/3), R(pi), R(4pi/3), R(5pi/3) }

The group has order 6 and is isomorphic to Z/6Z.  It is the rotational
symmetry of a regular hexagon (without reflections; reflections are not
enforced here because the branching angles break mirror symmetry).

Real snowflakes:  Ice Ih crystallises in the hexagonal crystal system
(point group 6mm, or D6h in three dimensions).  The six-fold rotational
symmetry of ice crystal growth is a consequence of the hexagonal
arrangement of water molecules in the basal plane.  In our simplified 2D
model, we capture the C6 rotational component without enforcing the
additional mirror symmetry, which is why each arm can have asymmetric
sub-branches.

Fractal dimension:  The recursive branching structure is a form of random
L-system.  The branching factor is 4 (two mid-branches + two tip-branches)
and the shrinkage factor averages about 0.5, giving an estimated fractal
(Hausdorff) dimension of approximately:

    D = log(4) / log(1/0.55)  ~  2.3

Clipped to the plane, the effective dimension is at most 2, but the
self-similar structure creates rich multi-scale detail.

The seed-locking trick:  By resetting the PRNG to the same seed before
drawing each of the 6 arms, the algorithm guarantees that the identical
random choices are made for every arm, producing exact C6 symmetry despite
using random parameters internally.
