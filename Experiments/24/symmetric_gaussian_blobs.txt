SYMMETRIC GAUSSIAN BLOBS
========================

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
A dataset of 64x64 grayscale images of soft, cloud-like patterns formed
by summing random 2-D Gaussian blobs.  Each blob is placed on the right
half of the image and a mirror copy is added on the left, producing
bilaterally symmetric "ink-blot" images with smooth, continuous intensity
gradients — a continuous-tone counterpart to the binary random walk of
Experiment 7.


2. GENERATION ALGORITHM
-----------------------
For each image:

  1. Let  cx = size / 2  be the axis of symmetry.

  2. Choose  num_blobs  in  {4, ..., 12}.

  3. For each blob:
       - Centre:  (bx, by)  with  bx in [cx, size-3],  by in [3, size-3]
       - Width:   sigma  in  [3, 10]  pixels
       - Peak:    amplitude  in  [0.5, 1.0]

  4. For every pixel (x, y), accumulate:

       canvas(x,y) += amplitude * exp( -((x-bx)^2 + (y-by)^2) / (2*sigma^2) )
                     + amplitude * exp( -((x-(2*cx-bx))^2 + (y-by)^2) / (2*sigma^2) )

     The second term is the mirrored blob.

  5. Normalise the canvas to [0, 255] by dividing by the global maximum.


3. MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND
---------------------------
Symmetry group:  Z2  (bilateral symmetry about the vertical axis)

Each blob is a 2-D isotropic Gaussian:
    G(x, y; mu, sigma) = exp( -||(x,y) - mu||^2 / (2*sigma^2) )

The sum of Gaussians is a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) with equal
covariance matrices.  The bilateral constraint means the mixture has
2 * num_blobs components, arranged in mirror pairs.

Properties of the Gaussian kernel:
  - It is the unique function that is both radially symmetric and
    separable:  G(x,y) = G(x) * G(y).
  - It is the Green's function of the heat equation:
    G_sigma = fundamental solution of  du/dt = nabla^2 u  at time
    t = sigma^2 / 2.
  - The Fourier transform of a Gaussian is another Gaussian:
    FT[G_sigma] = G_{1/sigma}  (up to normalisation).

The smooth, overlapping nature of the blobs creates a low-frequency
image with gentle gradients.  This makes the dataset a good test for
whether an autoencoder captures global spatial structure (as opposed to
the high-frequency edges in curve-based datasets).

The effective dimensionality of the dataset is  4 * num_blobs
(bx, by, sigma, amplitude per blob), ranging from 16 to 48 real
parameters.  However, the normalisation step means amplitude ratios
matter more than absolute values, reducing the effective dimension by 1.
