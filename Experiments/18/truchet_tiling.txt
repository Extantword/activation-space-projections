TRUCHET TILING
==============

1. WHAT IS IT
-------------
A dataset of 64x64 grayscale images of Truchet tile mosaics.  The image
is divided into a grid of square cells, each containing two quarter-circle
arcs that connect midpoints of adjacent edges.  The tile orientation
(one of two types) is randomised on the right half of the image and
mirrored to the left, producing bilaterally-symmetric labyrinth patterns
with smooth, continuous curves.


2. GENERATION ALGORITHM
-----------------------
For each image:

  1. Choose a cell size:  cs  from  {8, 16}  pixels.
     Grid dimensions:  grid = size / cs.

  2. Assign tile types (0 or 1) for each cell:
       - For cells in the left half (gx < grid/2): assign randomly.
       - For cells in the right half: set to  1 - tiles[mirror_col, gy]
         (mirrored and flipped to maintain arc continuity across the
         symmetry axis).

  3. For each cell at grid position (gx, gy):

       Compute the pixel origin:  (x0, y0) = (gx*cs, gy*cs).

       Type 0 — arcs at top-left and bottom-right corners:
         Arc 1: quarter circle centred at (x0, y0), radius cs/2,
                 from angle 0 to pi/2.
         Arc 2: quarter circle centred at (x0+cs, y0+cs), radius cs/2,
                 from angle pi to 3*pi/2.

       Type 1 — arcs at top-right and bottom-left corners:
         Arc 1: quarter circle centred at (x0+cs, y0), radius cs/2,
                 from angle pi/2 to pi.
         Arc 2: quarter circle centred at (x0, y0+cs), radius cs/2,
                 from angle 3*pi/2 to 2*pi.

     Each arc is drawn as a 16-point polyline for smooth rendering.


3. MATHEMATICAL BACKGROUND
---------------------------
Symmetry:  bilateral (Z2) about the vertical centre axis, plus local
            arc-continuity constraints.

Truchet tiles (named after Sebastien Truchet, 1704) are among the
simplest examples of random tiling systems.  Each tile has exactly two
orientations, and the arcs on adjacent tiles connect seamlessly regardless
of orientation, guaranteeing that every random assignment produces
continuous, non-intersecting curves.

The curves formed by connected arcs are closed loops of varying size.
The statistics of these loops (number, length distribution) are related
to percolation theory on the dual lattice.

For a grid of  N x N  tiles, there are  2^(N^2)  possible configurations
in the unconstrained case.  Our bilateral constraint reduces this to
2^(N * ceil(N/2))  configurations.

The quarter-circle arcs have curvature  kappa = 2/cs  (constant within
each arc).  The resulting global curves are piecewise-circular with
curvature discontinuities at cell boundaries — a C^0 but not C^1
assembly.

Parameter space:  For an 8x8 grid, each image is determined by
8 * 4 = 32 binary tile choices (right half), giving  2^32  ~ 4 billion
possible patterns.  For a 4x4 grid, 4 * 2 = 8 choices, giving 256
patterns.
